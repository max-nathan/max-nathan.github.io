Max Nathan

Professor of Economic Geography at University College London

I’m Professor of Economic Geography at UCL. I’m also an affiliate in the Urban Programme at the Centre for Economic Performance, and affiliated at CAGE and IZA@LISER. I’m a member of the Economic Geography editorial board.

My academic research has four broad themes. I work on the economics of cultural diversity, in particular the performance of diverse cities, communities and teams: I recently led a multi-year ESRC-funded programme exploring these connections. I also work on innovation systems and clusters, especially in tech; the creative industries and their impacts on neighbourhoods and cities; and public policy for cities, especially programme design and evaluation.

Outside academia, I co-founded the Centre for Cities think tank and the What Works Centre for Local Economic Growth. I’ve also worked in government in the UK Department of Communities and Local Government (now MHCLG) on issues like localism, regeneration and economic development.

Research themes

The economics of cultural diversity

Diverse cities, communities and teams; immigration, innovation and entrepreneurship.

Innovation systems

The microfoundations of innovation, and why proximity matters in workspaces, teams and clusters.

The creative industries and cities

Mapping creative clusters, and the impacts of creative activity on neighbourhoods and cities.

Public policy for cities

Urban economic development strategies, policy evaluation and urban governance.
