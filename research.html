Research

I’m an economic geographer who uses a lot of economics. I have four main research areas.

The economics of cultural diversity

I look at the economic performance of diverse firms and cities, and links between immigration, diverse communities, innovation and entrepreneurship. My PhD was about this. With colleagues from UCL, LSE, Birmingham, Toronto and Milan, I recently completed a multi-year ESRC-funded programme on these topics.

Recent work: Migration, diversity and firm performance; Migrant entrepreneurs, high-growth firms and cities; the 2017 gender pay gap legislation; GRAPH-EE.

Innovation systems

I explore the microfoundations of innovative activity and the importance of proximity in supporting ideas creation and flow, in workspaces, teams and clusters.

Recent work: The New Wave: technology diffusion in the UK; The economics of blitzscaling; Identifying emerging industries; creative clusters and creative multipliers.

The creative industries and cities

I’m interested in mapping creative clusters, and looking at the impacts of creative activity on surrounding neighbourhoods and cities.

Recent work: the BBC’s move to Salford; creative clusters and creative multipliers; don’t shoot the pianist.

Public policy for cities

Drawing on my policy background and past role in the What Works Centre, I’m look at urban economic development strategies, policy evaluation methods, and forms of urban governance.

Recent work: the BBC’s move to Salford; No 10 North; evaluating the Tech City programme.
